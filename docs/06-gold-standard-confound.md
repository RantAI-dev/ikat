# The gold standard is 80% anchor-derived — and it inverted our conclusion

Found 2026-08-10, while designing a follow-up experiment. This is
paper-blocking: it does not merely add a caveat, it reverses a headline claim.

## What happened

`generate-questions.ts` assigns gold figures two different ways:

| source | links | rule |
|---|---|---|
| span questions (`factual`, `explanatory`, `comparison`) | **332 (80%)** | figures whose **anchor** falls inside the gold span |
| `figure_dependent` | 85 (20%) | the figure the question was written from |

So four out of five gold links are *defined by the anchor*. Anchor-based
selection was being scored against a standard built out of anchors.

## The effect, measured

Selection F1 on `ugm3-built`, 229 questions carrying a gold figure:

| system | anchor-derived gold (n=144) | **clean gold (n=85)** | all (n=229) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `anchor_hybrid` | **0.327** | 0.182 | **0.282** |
| `anchor` | 0.326 | 0.138 | 0.274 |
| `co_embed` (descriptions) | 0.089 | **0.318** | 0.159 |
| `sel_gated` | 0.106 | 0.236 | 0.157 |
| `caption_match` (production) | 0.029 | 0.129 | 0.060 |

On the clean subset, description-based selection beats the anchor decisively —
hit@q 0.635 vs 0.224, McNemar **p = 7.9e-8**. The aggregate says the opposite
only because the anchor-favourable subset is four times larger.

## What this retracts

The claim *"every method that relies on descriptions loses to a rule that never
looks at the picture"* was reported with confidence. It is an artefact of the
gold construction, not a property of the methods, and it must not appear in the
paper in that form.

The finding was in fact already known and written into `systems.ts` when
`anchor_hybrid` was built — *"S4 lost 3x to S2 on the questions only a figure
can answer, and the cause was structural"*. Aggregate numbers dominated by the
span subset buried it.

## What survives

- **A1 still beats production by a wide margin on every split** (all: 0.274 vs
  0.060; clean: 0.138 vs 0.129 — narrow here, and honesty requires saying so).
- The **placement** results are untouched: they hold selection fixed, so the
  gold-construction bias cancels between the systems being compared.
- `anchor_hybrid` is the best system overall and the most balanced across
  splits. It should be the shipping candidate, not plain `anchor`.

## Both subsets are biased, in opposite directions

Saying only that the span subset favours the anchor would be half the story.

- **Span questions** favour the anchor by construction, as above.
- **`figure_dependent` questions** were generated by a model that saw the
  figure's surrounding text and was told to write something answerable *only*
  from the picture. Such questions naturally foreground visual content, which is
  exactly what descriptions encode. They likely over-represent
  "describe-the-picture" queries relative to what a student actually asks.

Neither split is a neutral referee. The defensible presentation is to report
both, state each bias, and refuse to pick whichever one flatters us.

## The hybrid recommendation, tested

Paired McNemar on hit@q, by split:

| comparison | all (229) | clean (85) | anchor-derived (144) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `anchor` vs `anchor_hybrid` | .384 → **.437**, p < 0.001 | .224 → **.353**, p = 0.001 | .479 → .486, p = 1.000 |

`n10 = 0` on every split: the hybrid never loses a question the anchor wins.

That is **partly structural and must not be oversold** — the hybrid takes the
anchored figures first and only fills leftover slots by similarity, so by
construction it can add but never remove. The hit@q test is therefore biased in
its favour.

What makes it a real win is that the added recall is **not paid for in
precision**: 0.232 vs 0.234 overall, effectively identical. A filler rule that
spent its spare slots badly would show up here as a precision drop, and it does
not.

Note on the record: RUN-STATUS lists "hybrid > anchor (p = 0.468, did not
replicate)". That was **GF-F1 including placement** on run v8. This is selection
alone (hit@q). Both can be true at once, and the earlier retraction stands.

## What has to happen before submission

1. **Report split results everywhere**, never the aggregate alone.
2. **A gold standard not derived from either mechanism.** The only clean source
   is human annotation: given a question and a page, which figure belongs? That
   also settles the LLM-judge limitation already recorded in §7.
3. **Re-examine every selection claim** in the manuscript against the split.

## Consequence for the product

The recommendation changes from "ship A1 plain" to **"ship `anchor_hybrid`"** —
it matches the anchor where the anchor is strong and recovers most of the
description advantage where it is not, and it wins on the aggregate as well
(0.282 vs 0.274).

The earlier advice to drop the description path entirely would have been a
mistake, and this document exists so that reasoning is not repeated.
