Placement Is Provenance: Anchor‑Preserving Ingestion for Figure‑Grounded Retrieval‑Augmented Generation

Where a figure belongs in an answer is decided by the document's author, and every layout parser reads it: the figure's position in the block sequence. Conventional ingestion throws that ordering away, then spends the rest of the pipeline guessing it back from captions — which four figures in five do not have in our corpus. IKAT keeps it instead. The anchor is a join over data the parser already produced: no model, no training, nothing tuned per corpus — and it makes every uncaptioned figure reachable.

Read this first — what is claimed, and what is not

This paper claims

  • Placement is separable from retrieval, cheap to measure, and decided at ingestion — the ground truth comes from the source typesetting, at zero annotation cost.
  • The anchor carries the signal. A 568M cross-encoder over anchor context tops every published system on MRAMG Academic selection; widen the window past the anchor and precision decays monotonically.
  • Placement does not need the generator. Judged blind, a mechanical anchor join ties an LLM's own inline placement exactly (48 questions to 49) — while staying traceable to a source position.
  • Absolute score floors do not transfer across corpora; any scale-free admission rule recovers 16 macro-F1 points. Pre-registered, then replicated out-of-sample.

It does not claim

  • Not across-the-board SOTA. MRAMG Lifestyle loses (55.00 vs 62.23); MMDocRAG trails GPT-4.1 (68.91 vs 80.7); with a small generator, inline insertion beats post-hoc selection on end-to-end F1.
  • Not a better ranker. Where descriptions are author-written, the cross-encoder ties BM25. The win is the representation it ranks, not the model.
  • Not statistical significance on the headline margin. +3.84 on 200 questions: bootstrap says "higher", not "significantly higher".
  • Not a product capability. Benchmark numbers are benchmark numbers; the stress corpus is far harder.

How it works

Two halves. Ingestion runs once per document and invokes no model at all. Serving adds two ranking stages per query.

Ingestion — once per document no model
1
Parse

The layout parser returns an ordered sequence of typed blocks per page: heading, paragraph, caption, figure, table. This ordering is the only input the method needs, and every layout parser already produces it.

2
Chunk

Prose blocks are segmented into retrieval chunks, each recording which block indices it spans.

3
Anchor

For each figure, find the nearest preceding prose block; the anchor is the index of the chunk containing it. Resolved by word-prefix match — first ten words, shortened to four until unique. One pass over blocks.

4
Persist

The anchor is written to the figure record and mirrored into chunk metadata, so retrieving the chunk recovers the figure by lookup instead of by search.

Serving — per query
5
Retrieve

Hybrid retrieval returns the top-k chunks for the query.

6
Admit

Every figure whose anchor chunk is in that set becomes a candidate. This is a join, not a caption test — which is the only reason the 66–81% of figures with no caption are reachable at all. No candidate enters by any other route.

7
Prefilter

A cross-encoder scores query against figure text and keeps the top 2. Two candidates match the full candidate set at a third of the latency.

8
Gate

Each survivor is shown as an image to a vision-language model under a strict yes/no prompt. The gate can only remove, never add or reorder, so its worst case is the prefilter's output.

9
Emit

Survivors are ordered by the stage-7 score and the top one is returned. Emitting nothing is valid and frequent.

10
Place

The figure is inserted at the sentence boundary its anchor points to, not at a position inferred from its caption.

Steps 3 and 6 are the contribution. Steps 7–9 compose existing components; the paper reports what each one adds.
